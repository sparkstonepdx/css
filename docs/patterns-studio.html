Studio site sections

Sections from sparkstonepdx.com, rebuilt in the class build with the site's own structure and copy. Images are left out: where the site shows a photo, these show the same block the site uses for a post with no image. The rules behind them are on Composition.

Header

The logo on the left, the section links and the booking link on the right. On the site the bar is sticky and blurs what scrolls under it; on a phone the links hide and a ghost menu button takes their place.

Same markup in both builds 
<header class="navbar">
  <div class="navbar-start">
    <a href="#" class="semantic" aria-label="Home"><strong>Sparkstone</strong></a>
  </div>
  <nav class="navbar-end">
    <a href="#" class="link">Case Studies</a>
    <a href="#" class="link">Products</a>
    <a href="#" class="link">Blog</a>
    <a href="#" class="link">Open Source</a>
    <a href="#" class="link">Book a call</a>
  </nav>
</header>
  • Layout: the site pushes the links right with an empty flex-grow spacer. .navbar-start and .navbar-end do the same with auto margins, so the spacer goes.
  • Actions: "Book a call" is a plain link in the header, the same weight as its neighbours. The filled button is saved for the hero.

Hero

A heading, one paragraph, one button. The site sets the heading at 1.5rem in its muted text colour, so it reads as a lead-in to the paragraph rather than a headline.

Same markup in both builds 
<section class="container prose" style="max-width: 80ch">
  <h1 class="text-secondary" style="font-size: 1.5rem">Custom software and websites for medical practices, law firms, and specialty contractors in Portland.</h1>
  <p>I'm Adam. I run Sparkstone, a one-person software studio. I build the systems your business actually runs on: intake, scheduling, referral portals, client dashboards, and the sites that feed them. I work on a monthly retainer, so the person who built your systems is the one keeping them running.</p>
  <a href="#" role="button" class="btn">Book a call</a>
</section>
<section class="container prose" style="max-width: 80ch">
  <p>Featured on <a href="#" class="link">Behind the Creative Curtain</a>, <a href="#" class="link">NarwhalPod</a>, and <a href="#" class="link">Creative Mornings</a></p>
</section>
  • Layout: every section on the site is its own .container capped at 80ch, the reading measure, so sections stack with nothing wrapping them.
  • Text: .prose sits on each container. It spaces the heading, paragraph and button, and gives each container a bottom margin to the next.

Services

Four services as cards in a wrapping row, then a card that sends anyone still unsure to a call.

Same markup in both builds 
<section class="prose">
  <h2>Services</h2>
  <div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(100%, 20rem), 1fr)); gap: 1rem; margin-bottom: 1rem">
    <article class="card">
      <h3>Custom Applications</h3>
      <p>the internal tool or portal you can't buy off the shelf</p>
    </article>
    <article class="card">
      <h3>Project rescue</h3>
      <p>take over a build that stalled, or clean up after a vendor</p>
    </article>
    <article class="card">
      <h3>Fractional CTO</h3>
      <p>support for teams that need direction, architecture, or product leadership without full-time overhead.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="card">
      <h3>Technical review</h3>
      <p>a paid second opinion on a vendor's proposal before you sign</p>
    </article>
  </div>
  <article class="card" style="text-align: center; padding: 2rem">
    <p>Not sure which one fits? Let's talk it through</p>
    <a href="#" role="button" class="btn">Book a consultation</a>
  </article>
</section>
  • Layout: a grid of columns at least 20rem wide, so the four cards sit two by two inside the 80ch measure and stack on a phone. min(100%, 20rem) stops a column overflowing a screen narrower than 20rem.
  • Surface: the call to action is the same .card as the services, centred, with double padding. The site also lays a faint radial highlight over it, left out here.
  • Spacing: the grid is a div, which .prose does not space, so it carries its own 1rem bottom margin, as on the site.

Quotes side by side, each attributed under its text.

  <h2>Testimonials</h2>

Post list

Case studies, products and blog posts share one list: each entry is a link wrapping an image and its title and summary. A post with no image shows its title in a tinted block instead, which is what these use.

Same markup in both builds 
<div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr; gap: var(--padding)">
  <a href="#" class="semantic">
    <article class="flex flip">
      <div style="flex: 1 1 30%; aspect-ratio: 1.5; display: flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; padding: var(--padding); border-radius: var(--border-radius); background: var(--surface-strong); color: var(--primary-text); font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold">The Map I Never Meant to Build</div>
      <div class="prose" style="flex: 1 1 70%">
        <h3 class="text-secondary">The Map I Never Meant to Build</h3>
        <p>Sixty thousand views, three neighborhoods asking to borrow it, and a little map that got a bit better every spring until it turned into something anyone can run.</p>
      </div>
    </article>
  </a>
  <a href="#" class="semantic">
    <article class="flex flip">
      <div style="flex: 1 1 30%; aspect-ratio: 1.5; display: flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; padding: var(--padding); border-radius: var(--border-radius); background: var(--surface-strong); color: var(--primary-text); font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold">A Library That Fits in a Free Little Library</div>
      <div class="prose" style="flex: 1 1 70%">
        <h3 class="text-secondary">A Library That Fits in a Free Little Library</h3>
        <p>A zine catalog, a printing guide, and an events page, built from a pile of draft copy in about two weeks.</p>
      </div>
    </article>
  </a>
</div>
  • Actions: the whole entry is one a.semantic, which strips the link styling. The title is in the muted text colour, as on the site.
  • Surface: on the site each entry is an article, which the classless build turns into a card. In the class build the article has no class, so the entries sit on the page, as here.
  • Boundary: the site tints the no-image block with its accent colour. In v3 that is a .retheme with --primary set to the accent around the block, if you want it to differ from the title.

About block

A photo beside a name, a role, and one line, with a link to the full page. The photo is a placeholder here.

Same markup in both builds 
<section class="prose">
  <h2>About Sparkstone</h2>
  <div class="flex flip">
    <div style="width: 8rem; aspect-ratio: 1; flex-shrink: 0; border: 2px solid var(--line); border-radius: 1rem; background: var(--surface-strong)" role="img" aria-label="photo of Adam Sparks"></div>
    <div style="max-width: 25rem">
      <h3 style="margin: 0">Adam Sparks</h3>
      <i class="text-secondary" style="display: block">Founder</i>
      <p>Everyone should have the freedom to build software they’re proud of without sacrificing joy, balance, or humanity.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <a href="#" class="link">About Sparkstone</a>
</section>
  • Layout: .flex .flip puts the photo above the text on a phone. flex-shrink: 0 stops the text squeezing the photo on a narrow screen.
  • Text: the role is .text-secondary and set to block so it sits on its own line under the name.

Newsletter

A heading, a sentence, one labelled field and a secondary button. It is a secondary button because the page's main action is booking a call.

Same markup in both builds 
<form class="prose">
  <h2>Newsletter</h2>
  <p>Subscribe to my newsletter to be the first to hear about what I do next</p>
  <label class="label">
    <p>Email</p>
    <input class="input" type="email" placeholder="Email" autocomplete="email" required style="max-width: 30rem" />
  </label>
  <button class="btn btn-secondary">Subscribe</button>
</form>
  • Controls: the .label spaces its text and field, and the field's own bottom margin spaces the button, so the form needs no layout of its own.
  • States: when the address fails, set aria-invalid="true" on the input and add a <small> after it. The label text and field turn to the error colour together.

    </section>
    <nav class="flex" style="flex-direction: column; align-items: end">
